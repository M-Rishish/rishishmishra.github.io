About

A microswimmer that reaches an oil–water interface has three futures: it passes through, it sticks, or it turns away. Which one it gets is decided in a few body lengths, by a competition between the flow the swimmer generates and the surface tension holding the boundary together.

My PhD, completed in the Department of Mechanical Engineering at IIT Jammu in June 2026, is about pinning that competition down. I run lattice Boltzmann simulations of squirmers in a Cahn–Hilliard diffuse-interface framework, on HPC clusters, and map the outcomes onto phase diagrams in dipole strength and capillary number.

Before the microswimmer work I studied conjugate heat transfer in machining and built automated rooftop harvesting systems — which is why I am as comfortable with a commercial CFD solver and a sensor rig as with a lattice code.

Why some swimmers get through

Interface crossing of prolate swimmers, separated into its thermodynamic and hydrodynamic parts. At the same swimming speed, the strength of the swimmer's force dipole decides the outcome.
